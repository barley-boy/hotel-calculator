How to calculate hotel occupancy rate

Divide rooms sold by rooms available and multiply by 100. Selling 300 rooms out of 400 available room nights equals 75% occupancy.

Why occupancy matters

Occupancy shows how much available inventory is being filled. It is most useful alongside ADR and RevPAR.

Occupancy vs. RevPAR

Higher occupancy does not automatically mean stronger revenue performance. A hotel can fill more rooms at lower rates and still produce weaker RevPAR.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good hotel occupancy rate?

There is no universal target. Compare your occupancy with similar hotels, seasonality, room rates, and operating costs.

Can occupancy exceed 100%?

Normally no. Rooms sold cannot exceed available room nights for the same inventory and period.

How does occupancy affect RevPAR?

When ADR is constant, RevPAR equals ADR multiplied by occupancy expressed as a decimal.

Hotel occupancy rate formula

Occupancy rate (%) = Rooms sold ÷ Available room nights × 100. Both inputs must cover the same period. Available room nights are the sum of rooms available for sale across the period, not just the number of rooms in the building.

Worked example: 80-room hotel over 30 days

If 80 rooms are available each night for 30 days, the hotel has 2,400 available room nights. If 1,800 room nights are sold, occupancy is 75%. At an ADR of $160, RevPAR is $120 (160 × 0.75).

Common mistakes

Do not divide total guests by total rooms. Use room nights sold, and keep the time periods consistent. If rooms are out of service, define your available-room inventory consistently with your reporting policy.
